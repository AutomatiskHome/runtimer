RunTimer

Interval timer for run-walk training on iPhone and Apple Watch.

What is RunTimer for?

In run-walk training you alternate between short running segments and walking breaks on a fixed schedule – for example, run for 2 minutes, walk for 1 minute. This makes longer distances manageable and easier on your joints, whether you are just starting out, returning after a break, or want to pace a long run deliberately.

RunTimer does the counting for you: set up your workout on iPhone, and your Apple Watch guides you through every phase with haptics and voice announcements – no need to look at your watch while running. The workout is saved to Apple Health as a complete activity.

Features

Training your way

  • Warm-up, intervals, cool-down: freely adjustable run and walk times, with a warm-up before and a cool-down after.
  • Fixed repeats or a goal: a set number of intervals – or intervals repeat until a time or distance is reached.
  • Total duration is calculated in advance and counted down during the workout.
  • Templates: classic interval templates, plus your own templates you can save, edit and pick right on the watch.

Guided through every phase

  • Haptics and voice announcements at every change: warm-up, run, walk, cool-down.
  • Countdown before each change – "5, 4, 3, 2, 1" spoken, with haptics for the last three seconds.
  • Progress: halfway, two intervals to go, last interval.
  • Kilometre announcements with your time for each full kilometre.
  • Every announcement can be turned off individually; volume is adjustable.
  • Announcements in English or German – automatically matching your watch language, or set manually.

Target zone for heart rate or pace

  • Set a heart rate or pace range for the running intervals.
  • If you are outside it for 10 seconds, the watch tells you to slow down or speed up, or that your heart rate is high or low – with a gentle directional tap.

Automatic pause

  • Stop – at a traffic light, for example – and the workout pauses automatically, then resumes as soon as you move again.
  • Detection combines step detection, GPS and motion sensors and works even with your arm hanging down.

Flexible on the go

  • Next phase: end the warm-up early, or cut the intervals short and go straight to the cool-down – with a tap or a double tap of thumb and index finger.
  • Keeps recording: after the programme ends, recording continues until you end the workout, so your full training volume is saved.
  • No iPhone needed: the workout runs entirely on Apple Watch.
  • Music at hand: the Now Playing page in the workout controls music or podcasts – play, skip and adjust the volume with the Digital Crown, without leaving the app.

Results

  • Apple Health & Fitness: heart rate, calories, distance, pace and GPS route.
  • Summary on watch and iPhone – with values split by running and walking phases (time, average pace, average heart rate).
  • Route on a map in the iPhone summary – colored by warm-up, run, walk and cool-down. Optionally without the first and last 100 m, so your start and finish locations stay private.
  • Workout history: all workouts on iPhone with a detail view – run and walk phases separately, goal achievement, route, upload status per service and the TCX file to share.
  • Share image: map with route, distance, time, pace, intervals and heart rate as an image – for messages, social media or your training diary.
  • Live status on iPhone during the workout – also on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island: phase, remaining time counting down, interval, heart rate, distance and workout time.
  • Workout file (TCX) for every saved workout – with each running and walking phase as a separate lap, heart rate and GPS route. Share it, save it to Files or upload it to training platforms such as Strava.

Automatic upload (optional)

  • Straight to your training platform: RunTimer can automatically upload every saved workout to Intervals.icu, Tredict or Runalyze – with each running and walking phase as a separate lap, heart rate and GPS route.
  • Easy to set up: connect Intervals.icu by signing in to your account; for Tredict and Runalyze, enter a personal API token. RunTimer never receives your password.
  • In the background: as soon as the workout file reaches your iPhone, it is uploaded – also over mobile data, with automatic retries if the connection drops briefly.
  • Optional: each service is off by default and switched on individually under Verbindungen (Connections). Only workouts from the time of activation are uploaded.

Privacy

  • No server, no ads, no tracking. Your data stays on your iPhone, Apple Watch and in Apple Health – unless you switch on an upload to a training platform yourself. See the Privacy Policy.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need for RunTimer?

An iPhone with iOS 26.2 or later and a paired Apple Watch with watchOS 26.2 or later. You set up intervals on iPhone or directly on the watch; the workout itself runs on Apple Watch.

Can I record a workout with just my iPhone?

No. Workouts are recorded only on Apple Watch – that is where the interval timer, announcements, heart rate measurement and automatic pause run. Without an Apple Watch you cannot use RunTimer for a workout. The iPhone is used to set up intervals and templates, and shows the live status during and the summary after the workout.

Do I need to take my iPhone with me?

No. The workout runs entirely on the watch, including GPS route, heart rate and announcements. With your iPhone nearby, you also see the live status there.

Why does the app ask for Health, Location and Motion access?

Health for heart rate, calories and distance during the workout and to save it. Location for distance, pace and the GPS route. Motion & Fitness for step detection used by the automatic pause. All data stays on your devices unless you switch on an optional upload.

The automatic pause doesn't react as expected – what can I do?

Check that Location and Motion & Fitness are allowed for RunTimer (iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security). Without GPS reception, e.g. indoors, detection relies on steps and motion only and reacts a little more slowly. During an automatic pause you can always tap ▶︎ to continue yourself.

Where can I find my workouts?

In the Fitness app and the Health app on iPhone – just like workouts from Apple's Workout app, including a map of your route.

How do I upload workouts automatically to Intervals.icu, Tredict or Runalyze?

In the iPhone app, open Verbindungen (Connections) and choose the service. For Intervals.icu, tap Mit Intervals.icu verbinden (Connect to Intervals.icu) and sign in there. For Tredict and Runalyze, create a personal API token in the service's settings (Tredict: activityWrite permission; Runalyze: activity_push with an expiry date) and paste it into RunTimer. Then switch on Trainings automatisch hochladen (Upload workouts automatically). You can follow progress under Übertragungsstatus (Transfer status).

Note: Tredict tokens only stay active if you sign in to Tredict at least every ten days; Runalyze tokens expire on the selected date and must then be replaced.

How do I upload a workout manually, e.g. to Strava?

After each saved workout, the watch transfers a TCX file to your iPhone. You'll find it in the iPhone app under Trainings (Workouts). Open the workout, tap Trainingsdatei teilen (Share Workout File), save the file to Files or email it to yourself, then upload it on the platform's website via "Upload". Each running and walking phase appears there as a separate lap.

How do I get the workout on my iPhone's Lock Screen?

Open RunTimer on your iPhone before starting the workout on your watch – or briefly once during the workout. The live status then appears on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island and stays up to date until the workout ends, even with your iPhone locked. iOS only allows such a Live Activity to start while the app is open. You can turn it off in iOS Settings under RunTimer → Live Activities.

Can RunTimer start my playlist automatically?

No – watchOS only allows this for Apple's own Workout app. Start your playlist in the Music app on your watch or with Siri (“Play my running playlist”) before the workout. During the workout, control it on the Now Playing page (scroll all the way down with the Digital Crown); ▶︎ resumes what you last listened to. Voice announcements briefly lower the music.
